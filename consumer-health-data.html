Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy

Mila · Updated 2026-10-29 · Privacy policy

About this policy

Effective 29 October 2026. This Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy explains how Mila collects, uses and shares consumer health data and how you can exercise your rights. It is written for consumers covered by the Washington My Health My Data Act (RCW 19.373) and Nevada's consumer health data law (NRS 603A.400 to 603A.550). "Mila", "we" and "us" means the developer of the Mila app, a sole trader based in Sweden; see the service provider details in our general Privacy Policy, which describes all of Mila's processing in more detail: https://milacoach.app/privacy-en.html.

1. Consumer health data we collect and why

Consumer health data means personal information that is linked or reasonably linkable to you and that identifies your past, present or future physical or mental health status. For Mila, that covers: fitness and workout data (workouts with distance, also for cycling, swimming, rowing and skiing, duration, pace, elevation, heart rate, running-form metrics, the route and Apple's effort score), to analyse your training, plan your sessions, write your coach texts, forecast race times and track shoe wear; heart, recovery and sleep data (heart rate, resting heart rate, heart-rate variability, heart-rate recovery, VO₂max, sleep and sleep stages with the times you fell asleep and woke up, blood oxygen, respiratory rate, sleeping wrist or skin temperature, and readiness or recovery scores from a connected service), to judge your recovery and adjust your training load, including how each night's values compare with your own usual level, worked out on your phone; body measurements and demographics (weight, height, body composition, age calculated from your date of birth, and biological sex), to set training zones and loads, estimate energy needs and compare race forecasts with others of your age and sex; activity and nutrition (steps and daily activity, walking data, food you log in other apps, and meals you photograph or describe in Mila with the estimated nutrition values), to assess your everyday load and fuelling; menstrual cycle data, only if you turn on the separate option to let the coach take your cycle into account (periods, cycle deviations in Apple Health and symptoms you log), read and summarised on your phone and never stored by Mila, to tailor your coaching; running-form images (still frames taken on your phone from a running-form video you choose, cropped around you with every face made unrecognisable before they are sent, and deleted once the analysis is written), to write a running-form analysis; what you tell us about your health and training (your description of yourself and your goal, notes, perceived effort, what you tell the coach, what you write to the coach, pain you report after a session and your answers to the coach's follow-up questions about your body, and messages to support), to understand your situation, adapt your plan and answer you; and inferences (the coach's texts, plans, analyses, recovery patterns, a coach profile derived from your description, running-form and race analyses, meal assessments, and a short follow-up of a niggle or of your return after illness or injury, including whether the coach advised you to seek care), to give you coaching that builds on what came before. The coach never makes a diagnosis. We collect consumer health data only to provide the Mila coaching service you ask for, to keep it secure and prevent misuse, and to comply with the law. We do not sell consumer health data. We do not use it for advertising or marketing, we do not share it with data brokers, and we do not use it to train AI models, nor allow our AI service provider to do so.

2. Sources of consumer health data

You, through what you enter, write, photograph or choose in the app. Apple Health on your iPhone and Apple Watch, only for the data types you allow in iOS; the data is read and analysed on your phone and only a summary is sent to our servers. Polar, only if you connect it and only for the data types you approve there. Workout files you import yourself (FIT, GPX or TCX). And Mila's own analysis of the above, which produces the inferences described in section 1.

3. Consumer health data we share, and with whom

We do not share consumer health data with third parties, and we have no affiliates. To run Mila, we disclose consumer health data to processors, companies that process it only on our behalf, under contract, and only to provide Mila to you: a cloud hosting and database provider in the European Union (Ireland), which stores your account and the data listed in section 1; an external AI service provider in the United States, acting as our processor, which receives the training and health summary (with the short cycle summary if you turned that option on), your description and goal, your notes and messages, meal photos and running-form still frames, only after you have agreed to the coach in the app and only to write your coach texts and answers; it may not use your data for its own purposes or to train its models, and it keeps the data only for a short time under its contract with us before deleting it; and an e-mail delivery provider in the United States, which delivers to our support inbox the support conversations you ask to send to a person. Apple provides sign-in, Apple Health and subscription payments on your device, and notifications reach your phone through Apple's push notification service; notification texts never contain health measurements. Images and texts you share yourself go only where you send them, and a shared running-year page never includes heart rate, weight or other health measurements.

4. How we ask for your consent

Before Mila collects consumer health data, the app asks for your consent on the screen Your health data and tells you what it collects and why. Before anything is sent to the AI service, Mila asks for a second, separate consent; it is voluntary and you can give it later, but without it the coach writes no plans, calls or answers, which are what the Mila Coach subscription is made of. Menstrual cycle data is read only after a third, separate choice that is off until you turn it on. You can withdraw consent at any time: the health data consent in the app under More → Account → Your health data; the coach under More → About you and your goal → Coach settings → The coach and AI (both also under Your consents on the subscription page the app shows without a subscription); the cycle option in the same place as the coach; Apple Health access in the iOS Health app or in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Health → Mila; and everything by deleting your account under More → Account → Delete account or on the subscription page. After you withdraw consent, we stop collecting or sending the data it covered.

5. Your rights and how to use them

You have the right to confirm whether we collect, share or sell your consumer health data, to access it, and to get a list of all third parties and affiliates with whom we have shared or sold it, with a way to contact them (we have none; see section 3); to withdraw consent to our collection and sharing of your consumer health data (see section 4); to have your consumer health data deleted: deleting your account in the app removes your data from our servers at once, and for a request made another way we delete within 30 days of confirming it is you; either way we notify our processors, which must delete it too, and copies in our hosting provider's backups are overwritten as the backups roll over and never later than six months after your request; and not to be discriminated against for using these rights. The fastest way is in the app: delete your account, withdraw consent or edit your data there. For any other request, use the contact form at https://milacoach.app/support.html, choose the topic Privacy / complaint and start your message with "US health data request". So that nobody else can get your data, we confirm the request through your Mila account, for example by asking you to confirm it from the support chat in the app while you are signed in, and we ask for no more information than we need. We answer within 45 days of receiving your request; if we need more time, we tell you within those 45 days and may extend the period once by up to another 45 days. Requests are free of charge, at least twice in any 12 months. If we decline to act on your request, you can appeal by replying to our answer or by using the contact form with the subject Appeal; we answer an appeal in writing within 45 days and explain what we did or did not do and why. If we deny your appeal, you can contact your Attorney General: Washington at https://www.atg.wa.gov/file-complaint and Nevada at https://ag.nv.gov/Complaints/File_Complaint/.

6. Additional information for Nevada consumers

The effective date of this policy is given at the top of the page. You can see and change most of your data directly in the app (your description and goal, workouts you added, notes, shoes and races); to review or ask us to correct anything else, use the contact form as described in section 5. We will tell you about material changes to this policy in the app and on this page before they take effect, and we will ask for your consent again before we collect or use consumer health data in a new way or for a new purpose. No third party can collect consumer health data about you over time and across different websites or online services through Mila: the app contains no advertising or analytics code from third parties.
