What Mila reads on your phone

With your permission Mila reads health data from Apple Health: workouts (with their route, their distance also for cycling, swimming, rowing and skiing, and the effort score you or your Apple Watch gave them), heart rate, HRV (SDNN, and RMSSD where your watch records it), resting heart rate, VO₂max, sleep (with sleep stages, time awake and when you fell asleep and woke up), blood oxygen, respiratory rate, sleeping wrist temperature, weight, height, body composition, activity, walking data, nutrition you log in other apps (energy, protein, carbohydrates and fat), and your date of birth and biological sex (Mila uses your age, not the date). This data is read and analysed on your phone. Mila also reads which apps and devices in Apple Health the data comes from (for example a watch's app, a scale, a food app or Apple Watch) and when they last synced, to show your watches and what is missing under More → Watches and apps; this stays on the phone, except that the summary for the coach says which watches and apps the data comes from, what they provide and when they last synced. From the night values (sleeping wrist temperature, respiratory rate, resting heart rate, HRV and blood oxygen) Mila works out on your phone how each night compares with your own usual level, shown under Analysis → Recovery as “Last night”; if you have agreed to the coach, these comparisons go with the summary so the coach can see when several of them move together. It is never a diagnosis. Each run's analysis is kept on the phone (never in a backup, deleted when you sign out), so Mila need not read every run again in the background. Mila on your phone never writes to Apple Health. Only when you use race mode, Long run with Mila or Start strength in Mila on Apple Watch does the watch save the race or the session as a workout in Apple Health: a race or a run with its route, distance, active energy and heart rate, a strength session as strength training with its active energy and heart rate; the watch sends it nowhere else, and it reaches the coach like any other workout, through the summary. If you turn on sessions on Apple Watch, Mila puts the coach's runs in the Workout app on your watch; that happens on the phone and nothing about it goes through Mila's server. The same goes for the coach's sessions in your calendar if you turn that on: Mila writes only its own events to the calendar you choose and reads no others. If you also turn on Find a free time, Mila reads on the phone when you are busy in your calendars, only the start and end of your events and never what they are called or contain, to suggest a free time with good weather for today's session. The times are not stored and never leave the phone; the coach only gets the suggested time. If you turn on Add the week's sessions automatically, Mila makes a calendar of its own called Mila and puts the week's sessions there. Mila then reads on the phone when you are busy in your calendars during the week, only the start and end and never what your events are called or contain, to put the sessions at free times, and reads back only its own events in the Mila calendar to see whether you moved or deleted them. The times you are busy are not stored and never leave the phone. Which sessions you moved or deleted, and to which day and time, is kept on the phone.

Apple Watch and Siri

If you use Mila on Apple Watch (a complication on the watch face, the app opened in the last two weeks, or, during your first week with Mila, the app installed on the watch), have agreed to the coach and have Mila Coach, your iPhone asks for today's note in the background in the morning, once last night's sleep is in Apple Health, so the coach's call is on your watch before you open the app. It is the same summary as when you open Today. Between your iPhone and Apple Watch everything goes directly between your devices, not through Mila's server: today's and this week's sessions with the coach's call and last night in plain words, today's and tomorrow's runs as workouts for the Workout app, the race plan in race week, the question after a run and your answer, the coach's first sentence and stars about the run, and from the watch the day you last opened Mila on it. Tell your coach on Apple Watch: what you dictate is turned into text by watchOS and sent to Mila on your iPhone, which handles the words exactly as when you type them in the app (they are saved on your account and included in the summary to the coach). The words stay on the watch until your iPhone has received them, for at most two days. If your iPhone cannot pass them on yet (it is locked), they wait there until you open Mila on your iPhone, and a notification says so. If you ask Siri or Shortcuts about today's session, Mila answers from what is already on your iPhone or watch; nothing is sent to Mila's server or the AI service for it. If you tell the coach something with Siri or Shortcuts (“Tell your coach in Mila”, on your iPhone or watch), your words are handled exactly as when you type them in the app.

What leaves your phone

To write your coach texts, Mila sends a short summary of your training and health (for example weekly distance, recent workouts, form, recovery, sleep, blood oxygen, body measurements, age and sex, food you have logged, the weather forecast for today, tomorrow and the rest of the week and, if you turned on Find a free time, the free time suggested today) together with your own description of yourself and your goal to Mila's server. The summary of your health also includes each recent workout's effort score (your own or your watch's estimate) and distance, also for other sports than running, your HRV with the measure it is (SDNN or RMSSD), and last night's deep sleep, REM sleep, time awake and when you fell asleep and woke up, compared with your own usual nights. When you start, the coach may ask a few short questions about your goal; the questions are saved with the coach's texts on your account, and your answers go into your description, Fixed sessions, Body or the coach's memory on your phone. If you have agreed to the coach, a short summary of your runs over the last year (for example distance and number of runs, usual days and times of day, the longest run and how your pace on a usual distance has changed, worked out on your phone) is also sent before you have started Mila Coach, so the coach can write one sentence about your running on the page where you start Mila Coach; the sentence is stored on your account like other coach texts, the summary is not stored. For the text under Analysis → Performance the coach also gets your watch's running dynamics from Apple Health (cadence, stride length, ground contact time, vertical oscillation and power when your watch measures them) as weekly averages over the last twelve weeks and, for your longest runs, the first third of the run against the last third, with the number of strength sessions per week; all of it is worked out on your phone and sent as part of the summary. If you have run high above sea level in the last few weeks, the coach also gets your runs' approximate height above sea level: one typical value per period, rounded to the nearest 100 metres, worked out on your phone from the heights in your runs' routes. No coordinates, places or routes are sent, and the value is not stored on the server. If you have a run today and loops under My loops, the coach also gets a short description of your loops to pick today's loop: each loop's shape, length and climb, how many times and how many of them in the dark you have run it, and whether today's wind is against you or behind you on the way out and home, worked out on your phone. The loops' names, maps and where they go are never sent to the external AI service. The coach's pick is saved to your account with today's comment; if you choose another loop, that is kept only on your phone and goes along in the summary, so the coach learns what you prefer. If the coach moves today's session to the treadmill because of the weather or a trip, the treadmill session is saved to your account with today's comment; if you tap Outdoors anyway, that is kept only on your phone and goes along in the summary. On a trip your phone searches Apple Maps for parks near you; your location is then used only by Apple for the search and is never sent to Mila or the external AI service. The coach's tips and its suggestions for what you could write to it are saved to your account with today's comment; which tips you've seen or closed, and which sessions the coach changed after what you wrote, are kept only on your phone. If you write that you ran with someone, your coach may ask whether you'd like to invite them as a running buddy; Mila never compares where or when different runners run. The summary is used only to write the text: for texts written in advance (such as the weekly texts and the morning note) it waits in a queue until the text is written, at most about a day, and is then cleared. The coach texts, your description, your goal and a coach profile derived from it are stored on your account. Sessions you tick off in the plan are stored on your account with their date, minutes and perceived effort (1–5), so the coach and all your phones see them; they are deleted with the account. If you say that a workout was a particular planned session, or was not, that choice is stored on your account (the day, the session in the plan and which workout it concerns), so all your phones count your sessions the same way; it is deleted with the account. If you move a session, the moved plan is stored on your account, and the plan and the move are sent to the external AI service so the coach can check the week. With Add the week's sessions automatically, the week's plan, the week's forecast, your usual times to run (worked out from your workouts) and the sessions you moved or deleted in the Mila calendar, with day and time, are sent to the external AI service so the coach can place the week; if the coach moves a session, or you move one to another day in the calendar, the moved plan is stored on your account. Exercises you tick off in a strength session and your watch choices (No watch, and whether the warm-up, the main part and the cool-down go to Apple Watch with an open goal or with targets) are stored on your account and deleted with the account. Your choices in the app for appearance (More → Appearance), units (More → Units), the period the charts under Analysis show, your notification choices (the reminder about tonight's food, the Sunday reminder, the reminder to film your running form again, notifications from running buddies and groups, and My running year in December), whether you contribute elevation data to the race register, and whether you want your sessions on Apple Watch, in your calendar and the weather where you run are stored on your account when you change them, so they follow you to a new phone; they are deleted with the account. The permissions you give in iOS (for example for the watch, the calendar, your location and notifications) are never stored on your account and stay on each phone, so on a new phone you turn them on again with a tap. What you write to the coach in your own words under Tell your coach that the coach finds concerns the week, and the coach's short interpretation of it (what has happened, a short summary and for how many days it affects your training), are stored on your account until you delete the account, and are sent to the external AI service as part of the summary the coach writes from. To decide whether what you write under Tell your coach concerns today's session or the week, your words are first sent to the external AI service with a short description of today's session; this is not stored. What you write there that concerns today's session is sent to the external AI service in the same way, with the day's summary, and your words, the coach's answer and your choice (use the suggestion or keep the plan) are stored on your account as a coach text for that day; if you use the suggestion, today's session in your plan is replaced. The days you tick off under This week's focus are stored on your account per week, with the coach's focus and how many days it set, so all your phones see them and the coach sees how it went in the weekly review; they are deleted with the account. In the weekly review the coach puts you at one of seven places on Mila's mountain and writes a short line about you; the place, the line and when you reached each place are stored on your account as a coach text and deleted with the account. You can turn Mila's mountain off under More → Settings → Follow-ups. Fixed sessions you tell the coach about, for example club training or parkrun (weekday, time, rough length, kind of session, name, the coach's short note, the words you wrote and your own corrections), are stored on your account until you remove them or delete your account, and are sent to Mila's coach (an external AI service in the USA) when it lays the weekly plan and writes today's comment. If you photograph a training schedule, your phone reads the text in the picture itself: the picture never leaves your phone, and only the text is sent to the coach to read in the sessions, without being stored. If you add workouts from a screenshot or a photo of a screen (such as a treadmill's display), your phone reads the text in the picture itself: the picture never leaves your phone and is not stored, and only the text your phone read is sent to the coach to read out the workouts. Names of places and people are removed as far as possible (the map's place names already on your phone, and the coach disregards names and places), but this cannot be guaranteed. Shoes you add (name, date, distance, replacement limit and the coach's details on the model), which run was done in which shoe and which shoe was your default, notes you write on your workouts, and races you add to your plan with the courses you upload for them are stored on your account, so they follow you to a new phone; they are deleted with the account. If you remove a race from your plan, the race's name and date are stored on your account so it does not come back when the coach reads your description again; this is deleted if you add the race again, and with the account. Phrases you write to yourself for the hard parts of a race are stored on your account with the race, so they follow you to a new phone, and are sent to Mila on Apple Watch with the race's plan; they are deleted with the race or the account. They are also sent to the external AI service as part of the summary when the coach judges how ready you are for the race, so the coach's suggested phrases can build on your own words. If you connect Polar, Mila stores the access tokens and, for the data types you approve at Polar: training sessions (with heart rate, speed and route), sleep (time asleep, sleep stages and sleep score), Nightly Recharge (night-time HRV, heart rate, breathing rate and recovery status), continuous heart rate (used for your daily resting heart rate), daily activity (steps and active time), night-time skin temperature, test results (orthostatic test, fitness test, SpO2) and diary entries in your Polar calendar (weight, feeling, perceived recovery and your physical settings such as maximum heart rate, resting heart rate, thresholds and VO2max). From your Polar physical settings Mila stores only max HR, resting HR, thresholds, VO₂max and FTP; birthday, sex and height are not read or stored. Mila does not read your e-mail, devices or diary notes at Polar, and cannot send anything to your Polar watch. If you allow notifications, Mila stores your phone's push address, with the phone's language, time zone and region, to tell you when weekly texts are ready (the language and time zone set the notification's text and time of day; the region is used to count users per country). With Today's session on the Lock Screen on, Mila also stores your phone's address for starting the Lock Screen activity and the time you usually wake up (worked out on your phone from when you first open Mila in the morning and when your night ends in Apple Health), so that on a day with a session the activity can appear on your Lock Screen at that time. It comes without a sound or notification text, through Apple's push service with only today's session as your plan names it; turning the setting or notifications off removes both. The coach's notifications come between 08:00 and 21:00 on your phone; a reply from support (the AI support or a person at Mila) is sent as soon as it is ready, at any hour. For a race in your plan, Mila stores the race's name, date, start time and the coach's breakfast time on your account to send the race-week notifications (the evening before and on race morning); they are removed when the race has passed or left your plan, or when you turn race notifications off under More → Notifications, and are deleted with the account. If your goal has a time you gave it yourself, a date or a rough time frame, Mila stores the goal's day, whether it is a time frame, the day the time counts as passed and a one-way code of the goal's name (never the name) on your account, to send one notification once the time has passed (not if you turned the notification off under More → Notifications); this is removed when the goal has no time and is deleted with the account. If the coach identifies a course you upload from a file or link as an official race, it may become part of Mila's shared race registry after Mila has reviewed it, without your name: the course's line without its first and last 300–500 metres (so a recording that starts at home never shows where), its distance and elevation, and the race's name, date and organiser's page. Turn off Share the course with the race registry when you upload, and it never is. When you add a race by hand, Mila's server compares the race's name, date and distance and, if you uploaded a course, its start with Mila's race registry. If it is certainly the same race, the link is saved on your account and the race gets the registry's course; if you tap Not the same race, that is saved too, so the two are never linked again. None of this is sent to the external AI service, and the links are deleted with the race or the account. If you import workouts from files you exported yourself (FIT, GPX or TCX, for example Strava's archive), Mila reads the files on your phone and stores the workouts on your account: time, activity, distance, elevation gain, energy, heart rate (average, maximum and heart rate curve), speed and GPS route. The file itself is not sent to Mila's server and is not kept. Workouts you add manually are stored on your account with activity, time, distance, perceived effort (1–5) and your note. You can delete such a workout in the app; all of them are deleted with the account. Mila does not connect to Strava; workouts you record in Strava reach Mila through Apple Health or from files you export yourself.

Menstrual cycle (optional)

If you turn on "Let the coach take your menstrual cycle into account", Mila reads from Apple Health your periods (flow and cycle start), the cycle deviations Apple Health itself has found (irregular, infrequent or prolonged periods and persistent spotting) and symptoms you have logged, such as cramps, headache, fatigue, hot flashes and night sweats. This is a separate, explicit consent to processing health data, apart from your agreement to the coach, off until you turn it on, and it applies only to the phone where you turn it on. The data is read and summarised on your phone; when the coach writes your daily texts and answers, only a short summary (roughly which day of your cycle you are on, your usual cycle length, the deviations in Apple Health and today's and yesterday's symptoms) is sent to our external AI service in the USA, never the history or any dates. Mila does not store your cycle data, neither on your phone nor on our servers, beyond what the coach itself writes in its texts, which are stored like other coach texts. You can turn it off at any time under More → About you and your goal; Mila then immediately stops reading and sending the data and deletes what it had of it on your phone. You remove Mila's read access in the Health app under Sharing → Apps and Services → Mila. If you live in a US state with consumer health data laws, such as Washington's My Health My Data Act, your cycle data is consumer health data: Mila collects it only with your consent and only to tailor your coaching, shares it only with the AI service that writes the coach's texts on our behalf, never sells it, and you can withdraw your consent and request access or deletion at any time through the contact form at milacoach.app.

Subscription

If you subscribe to Mila Coach, Mila stores the App Store transaction id, the product, the status and the expiry date on your account to know whether the subscription is active. Apple sends Mila updates when the subscription renews, ends or is refunded. Payment details are handled by Apple and never reach Mila.

Meals and food

A meal photo is sent to the AI model for assessment and then discarded. Mila stores only the estimated numbers and text, never the photo. When you choose a photo, Mila reads when it was taken so the meal lands on the right day; the photo is sent scaled down without other metadata and is never saved. You can correct a logged meal in your own words, for example “half portion”. The correction is sent with the meal's saved parts, without any photo, to Mila's coach (an external AI service in the USA), and the meal is saved with the new estimate and your correction. With Siri or Shortcuts (“Log a meal in Mila”), what you say is sent as a description of the meal to Mila's coach, just as when you describe it in the app. Food you've logged in other apps that's in Apple Health (energy, protein, carbohydrate and fat per day) is added to your meals in Mila and included in the summary sent to the coach, including for the weekly review. From when you usually eat (your meals in Mila and the times of food from other apps in Apple Health) your phone works out your approximate usual meal times, which the coach gets together with the time of day so that its food advice fits the time of day; the time of each single meal in Apple Health never leaves your phone. If you choose Show without numbers, your food is shown without kcal and grams, and the coach then also writes about food without numbers. Log again copies an earlier meal without sending anything to the coach. The reminder about tonight's food is off until you turn it on under More → Notifications. It's a local notification on your phone with a generic text, never the coach's text, and it only comes when the coach has judged that tonight's food matters for tomorrow's session. Your own notes after long runs, for example about energy and your stomach, are part of what the coach uses for the weekly plan and the race's fuelling plan. In the weekly review the coach may say something brief about food when the pattern is clear over several logged days; it never suggests you eat less. Under Suggest the week's food the coach suggests a meal plan for your training week, either entirely on its own or based on what you have at home. If you choose the latter, you can photograph your fridge, freezer or pantry (one or a few pictures) and write what you have; the pictures are sent scaled down to Mila's coach (an external AI service in the USA), used only for the suggestion and never kept, neither in Mila nor on your phone. Your wishes and what you write about allergies and intolerances are sent with the same suggestion and kept only on your phone, so you don't have to write them again. The same goes for what you write about what you have at home: it is sent only with a suggestion based on what you have at home and kept only on your phone until you change or clear it. What you write about allergies and what you don't eat is also shown under What the coach remembers about you, where you can change or clear it; it is the same text, it is sent with the suggestions and with the recipes the coach writes, and it is kept only on your phone. On Today the coach can also suggest tonight's meal when its line about food is about the evening: under Tonight's food the coach writes a recipe for that meal, and under Food for the week you can show a recipe for each meal. The coach's line about food, or the meal from Food for the week, is then sent with the same kind of summary as for Food for the week, and if you choose From what I have at home, the same applies to the pictures as above. The recipe is stored on your account as a coach text, and recipes you choose to save are under Food → Saved recipes until you remove them or delete your account. The recipes the coach has written for you, including the earlier one when you ask for another suggestion, are kept on your account and listed under Food → Earlier suggestions until you delete your account; opening one there sends nothing to the coach. You can put what is missing on a shopping list that is kept only on your phone. You can also send the shopping list from Food for the week or a recipe to Reminders, where Mila writes it in the list “Mila – shopping” on your phone, or share it to Notes or another app; none of this is sent to Mila's server. The suggestion (the meals, a short reason for each day and the shopping list) is stored on your account as a coach text. These are suggestions, not medical advice. With Photograph it when it's done you can take a photo of a dish you cooked from a recipe. The photo is kept only on your phone, protected and outside backups, and shown instead of the recipe's picture until you delete it, sign in with another account or delete your account. It is sent nowhere unless you share it yourself or send it to the coach as a meal; then the same applies as for other meal photos. After a meal the coach may ask Cook it again? Your answer, what you write and what you do with the recipes (photographed, put on the shopping list, swapped) are kept only on your phone as a taste memory, which you can see, change and clear under Food, where you can also turn the question off. When your consent to the coach covers it, the taste memory and what is new in it are sent with the suggestions for the week's food and the recipes to Mila's coach (an external AI service in the USA), which rewrites the taste memory in its own words; it is not stored on your account. Recipes you answered Yes to are under Food → Our favorites on your phone, and the coach may put one of them into a busy week. A card about the year's food in Your running year is based only on dishes you answered Yes to or photographed; their names are sent with the year's reflection, and the card is shown only in the app, never on a page you share.

Shoes

If you import a shoe log under Shoes, the picture, or the text of the file you choose, is sent to the external AI service, which reads it to fill in your list of shoes. The picture is not stored, nor is the file's text; only the shoes you approve are stored on your account. If you photograph your shoes under Shoes, your iPhone first makes any faces in the photos unrecognisable, and the photos are then sent to the external AI service, which recognises the brand and model and may mention wear it sees. The photos are not stored by Mila; only if you choose it yourself is a photo kept as the shoe's picture, and then only on your iPhone. If you do not know how far a pair has run, the coach estimates it from your running since you bought the pair and from your other shoes, and the estimate is stored as the shoe's distance. To judge how long a shoe lasts for you, Mila also sends a summary of how the shoe is used (for example pace, distances and terrain of the runs in it), your weight, running dynamics from Apple Health when available, a summary of your latest running form analysis, what you have written about the shoe and the runs in it, and the shoe's age and distance. The coach's judgement and its reason are stored with the shoe on your account.

AI support

Messages you write to the support in the app are answered by an AI (an external AI service), and the answer says so. With your message the app sends a few technical details about the app and its connections (such as app version, iOS version and which services are connected), not your health data. The Mila team may read your support conversation, for up to 180 days, to help you and to improve the support. If the AI cannot help, or you ask for a person, a copy of the conversation is also e-mailed to Mila's support inbox. The conversation is stored on your account for 180 days and then deleted. If a person at Mila answers, the answer appears in the same conversation. When you delete your account, the conversation and the copies of it stored by Mila are deleted; a copy already e-mailed to the support inbox may remain there for up to 90 days. What you wish for under "Make a wish" is grouped with other runners' wishes into themes in a daily report to the Mila team, with counts and sometimes a short quote in your own words, but never with your name, e-mail address or account. A screenshot you attach to a wish is sent to the external AI service only to understand the wish and is never stored. The wish is stored on your account so the Mila team can reply when it has been done.

Tell your coach

What you write or say under "Tell your coach" is sent, with a summary of your training data, to an external AI service. It first decides whether it is a question, a change to today's session or to the week, and then writes the answer. If you talk instead of typing (the microphone in the field), what you say is turned into text on your phone; the audio never leaves your phone and is not stored, and only the text is sent, just as when you type. On Apple Watch you use the watch's own dictation, and Mila only receives the text. Questions and answers are kept on your phone (the latest 20) and deleted there when you sign out. Mila's server keeps your questions and the coach's answers for 26 hours, so the coach reads the conversation from its own answers and not from a copy that could be changed, and then deletes them; it also keeps the latest answer for about ten minutes, so it can be fetched again if the connection drops, and a count of how many questions you have asked. If the coach judges that what you write is about a crisis (thoughts of harming yourself, an eating disorder or an acute medical situation), it answers even when the week's questions are used up; the server then keeps only a one-way code (hash) of the words for 30 minutes, never the words. If you report one of the coach's answers (Report the answer), a copy of that answer is kept with the report so the Mila team can read it. What you write about today's session or the week is stored on your account with the coach's answer or reading and is included in the coach's texts while it applies. If you leave the app before the answer is ready, it is kept until the app has fetched it, at most two days, and you get a notification without the answer in it.

Your body

If you write that something hurts, after a session (under "Pain anywhere?", in the note or on the watch), to the coach or when you say you are injured, it is sent with the rest to the external AI service. If the coach judges it a niggle worth following, or follows your return after illness or injury, a short follow-up is stored on your account: the coach's line on how things stand, the place in your own words (for example left calf), the coach's questions and your answers, in your own words or one tap (at most the latest 14), and whether the coach has advised you to seek care. It is included in the coach's texts while it is open. Once it is closed, by the coach or by you, a short summary (the place, the dates, the coach's last line and whether you closed it yourself) is kept among at most 8 earlier follow-ups, shown under Analysis → Body and read by the coach for six months so it learns from them. Everything is deleted when you delete your account. The coach never makes a diagnosis.

Your patterns and the year in review

Once a month the coach looks for patterns in your own data under Analysis → Recovery. A compact summary of the last weeks is then sent to the external AI service, the same kind of data the coach already receives: per day and week your runs (time of day, distance, time, pace, average heart rate, elevation gain, perceived effort and your notes), sleep, HRV and resting heart rate, together with your answers on whether last month's patterns were right. Those answers are kept only on your phone. For the coach's reflection in My running year (in December, or for the previous year when you open it) a summary of the year is sent the same way: distance, time, elevation gain and number of runs per month, races with times, records, the most-used shoe and weeks in a row with running. The patterns and the reflection are stored on your account like other coach texts. Since you can share the reflection, the coach writes it about your training only: it never mentions injuries, illness, weight, heart rate, sleep or other health details.

Shared year pages (My running year)

If you choose Share my year, Mila stores what you chose to show (for example total distance, time and elevation gain, races with times, records, your favourite shoe and, if you turn it on, the coach's reflection, which is then taken on the server from what the coach wrote for the year and never mentions health details) and a picture of your year made in the app, at an address that cannot be guessed. Heart rate, weight and other health values are never included. Anyone with the link can see the page; it is not indexed by search engines. The page is served through Cloudflare at ar.milacoach.app, which sees visitors' requests but nothing about your account; the page loads no fonts, scripts or anything else from third parties, and is never kept in a cache. You can have at most three active links per year and can stop sharing at any time in the app; the page then stops showing at once, and the page and the picture are removed entirely within a few minutes. Everything is deleted when you delete your account. Once a year, from 1 December, you get a notification about your running year so far. If you share your year before it ends and leave this on, the shared page is updated to the full year when you open the app from 1 January, under the same link and with the same details you chose to show, never more. The coach's reflection on the page never changes by itself: if the coach writes a new one, the app shows it to you first, and it goes on the page only if you choose it.

Running form analysis

If you ask the coach to analyse your running form, your films are read on your phone and never leave it. You can film in the app or choose videos from Photos. A video you take in the app is recorded without sound, is not saved to Photos and is deleted from your phone as soon as it has been read. The phone takes out still frames at key moments of your stride (at most 8 per angle), together with estimated measurements, and sends them to Mila's server. Before anything is sent, your phone crops each still frame around you and makes every face unrecognisable, including other people who happen to be in view. With the frames, Mila also sends, when there is any and you want to: the run the video was taken during (distance, pace and indoors or outdoors, found from the video's date), the shoe you choose, your watch's running-form measures from your latest runs, what you write about pace and surface, what the coach should know and how the drills have gone, and your previous analysis with the phone's measurements then, plus the summary of up to three analyses before it, with what you corrected in them. The frames wait in a queue until the analysis has been written, usually within a day and at most a few days if it has to be tried again, and are then deleted at Mila; the batch with them is deleted at the AI service once Mila has collected the analysis. The written analysis is stored on your account so the coach can compare it with the next one. From the side, the phone makes up to two of the stills close-ups of your foot and shoe, cropped and handled the same way. If you write under an observation that it isn't right (Not right?), your comment is sent with the same stills and measurements again, directly to the AI service, which looks again; the stills are not kept by Mila, and the corrected analysis replaces the old one on your account. The still frames and the body points the phone found in them are also kept on your phone, only there and never in a backup, so you can see what the coach looked at and compare with the next analysis. You can delete them under Training → Around your running → Running form; they are also deleted when you delete your account or sign in with another account. A short summary of your latest analysis (date, summary, main observations and drills) goes with the coach's other texts for twelve weeks after the analysis. The analysis is a coach's observations, not a medical assessment, and does not replace a running analysis in a lab. After an analysis, for the weeks it applies, the coach's weekly review gets your watch's running dynamics from Apple Health (cadence, ground contact time and vertical oscillation as weekly averages on easy runs), so the coach can say whether the drills seem to be working; this is sent as part of the summary.

Registration and bib pickup

For races in Mila's race directory, Mila reads the dates the organizer has published on their own website (when registration closes, when the price goes up and when bibs are picked up) and shows them in the app and on the race's public page; these are facts about the race, not about you. So that the coach can judge whether and when a reminder is worth sending, Mila's coach (an external AI service in the USA) receives the race, the dates, your answer to whether you have registered and your changes to the reminders. So that Mila can send the reminders as notifications, even when you haven't opened the app, your account stores the coach's judgement, the reminders with their days and the dates they concern, when they were sent, your answer, your changes and whether the reminders are on. When the organizer changes the dates, the coach judges them again on the server. This data is deleted when the race leaves your plan and when you delete your account.

Before the race and Reminders

If you ask the coach for a packing list on a race's page, the race (name, date, distance and course), the organiser's details about the start, cut-off times and mandatory kit, the weather forecast for the start, the coach's fuelling plan and shoe choice for the race, roughly how far the race is from where you usually run (only a distance in kilometres, never where you live) and your coach profile are sent to Mila's coach (an external AI service in the USA); the list is stored on your account as a coach text. You can put the packing list, the race morning's timeline and the coach's suggestion of when to wind down the evening before an important day in Reminders. Mila then writes them only on your phone, in the lists “Mila – pack for <the race>”, “Mila – race morning” and “Mila – evening routine”, and changes or removes only what Mila itself put there (the race morning moves if the start time changes and is removed if the race leaves your plan). Mila reads no other reminders, and none of it is sent to Mila's server. The coach's suggestion to wind down rests on the sleep and training in the usual summary sent to the coach; nothing new is sent for it.

After the race

When a race in your plan has been run, the coach writes an analysis of it: the summary sent to the external AI service then also holds the race's course, your time, your kilometre splits and heart rate during the race, the coach's plan for the race, your own notes on the race (also your answer on the watch after the run), the day's weather at the start and your earlier times in the same race; the analysis is stored on your account with the other coach texts. Under My results, when there are new results, the coach writes a short text about all your races: your races (name, date, distance, official time and placing, the watch's time and the coach's lesson from each race) and the next race in your plan are then sent to the external AI service, and the text is stored on your account with the other coach texts. An official time and placing you enter yourself under My results is kept only on your phone and is included in that summary. Mila can find your official result in two ways. Only if you have chosen to let Mila fetch your official results automatically (asked once on a race's page or when you add a race, and under More → About you and your goal), Mila sends, after each race in your plan, the name you chose for results lists, with the race's name, place, date and distance, your watch's finish time for the race, your sex and age class from Apple Health and your club if you gave one, to the external AI service to find your own row in the organiser's or timing provider's public results list; Mila tries a few times in the two weeks after the race until the result is found. The name and the club are kept only on your phone and are not stored by Mila; you can change them or turn this off at any time. Without that choice nothing is sent unless you search yourself under Get my official result or Search yourself: then the name or bib number you type is sent, with the race's name, place, date and distance. The race always comes from your own race on your account, not from the app. The service first finds the race's results list from the race's own details, without anything about you, and then looks for your name or number only in that list's own pages; the name is never used in a web search. What you type, the name you chose and the other details are used only for that lookup and are not stored by Mila. If it is not certain which row is yours, up to three possible rows (time, placing and class only, never names) are shown on your phone for you to choose from; they are kept only on your phone until you choose. Mila stores only your result (official time, place overall and in your class, the class and the results page) on your account, never anyone else's, and it is deleted with the race or the account. When your result is found automatically you get a notification with the race's name, never more. A share image you make of a race or a workout is drawn on your phone and goes only where you send it. Heart rate, the coach's comment and the coach's stars for the run (when it has any) are included from the start and can be turned off before you share. The route coloured by pace or heart rate, and the route on the image of a record or a race, are only included if you choose them yourself for that image. On a workout's image the route is included from the start, as its line only, from start to finish, with no map, and you can turn it off before you share. An image of the heatmap in All routes is drawn on your phone too, with only the lines and no map, street names or places, and everything within about 500 metres of where your runs start and end is cut away, so your home does not show. The backdrop is drawn on your phone too: to choose the landscape (for example city, lake or coast), the phone looks up the place a little way into the route through Apple's location service, and only the answer is kept, on the phone. When you open sharing, Mila asks the coach for a suggested text to go with the image (only if you have agreed to the coach): only what the image shows is sent to the external AI service (activity, date, distance, time, pace, elevation, the race's name and place, the official time and placing, the days left and forecast before a race, and a new record), never heart rate or other health data. When Coach's comment is on, the coach's own comment on the run, or after a race its race analysis, is also sent as the basis for a short line from the coach on the image; the line never shows health details. An image of a finished week (Share the week) shows only that week's running; for its suggestion the week's number and dates, the running distance, the time and the number of runs, which other kinds of training you did and for how long, and, when Coach's comment is on, the weekly review's headline and summary are sent, never the plan or the weeks ahead. The suggestion and the line are stored on your account per image and language so they are not written again, and are deleted with the account. The text you share, which Mila also copies to the clipboard when you share, goes only where you send it.

Running buddy and races

Running buddy: once you and another Mila runner have both said yes, your buddy sees the name you chose for buddies and, while you share your week, how many sessions you have planned and done this week and how many weeks in a row you have followed the plan, and, for a race where you have turned on "Show me to other Mila runners in the race", whether you have the same coming race in your plans (yes or no, never which). You can have at most ten running buddies. Your buddy never sees health data, heart rate, HRV, sleep, weight, workouts, places, routes or the coach's texts. The coach may be told that you have running buddies and how many of them completed the week's planned sessions, and may mention it in the weekly review; never who they are or anything else about them. Only buddies who share their week and have agreed to the coach are counted, and only when there are at least three of them; otherwise the coach is told nothing about your buddies. On a race's page, Mila shows how many other Mila runners have the same race in their plan. The number is counted on the server without names and is only shown when it is at least five. If you turn on "Show me to other Mila runners in the race" (off by default), the name you chose for buddies is shown to other runners in the same race who also show themselves, and they can invite you to be running buddies. Nothing else about you is shown, and you can turn it off at any time. Cheers: buddies can send a ready-made message or up to 80 characters of their own text, and after a race runners who showed themselves can cheer each other once with a ready-made message. Cheers are kept for 30 days. The notification arrives between 08:00 and 21:00 and only says that someone cheered, never who or what. Your own text only goes between running buddies who have both said yes, without links, email addresses or phone numbers, and the number of cheers per day is limited. You can report a cheer to the Mila team, who then see what it said, and block the sender; a buddy request from a race's list, or a runner's name in a race's list, can be reported in the same way, and the team then sees the name; the person you block is not told and can no longer invite you, cheer you on or see you in a race's list. You can end a buddy pairing at any time. Running together: while you have a running buddy and share your week, the app sends your coming easy runs and long runs (the day and the kind of session, for today and the next seven days, nothing else about the sessions) to Mila's server. Your buddy never sees them; the server only says which days you both have the same kind of session, and then the coach may ask whether you would like to suggest running together. A suggestion to your buddy holds only the day, the kind of session and, if you choose, morning, lunch or evening; your buddy gets a notification without details and answers yes or no, and you are told of a yes with a notification. At most three suggestions a day and one per buddy and day, never to or from someone you have blocked. The days and the suggestions are deleted the day after the session's day, when you stop sharing your week, or when the pairing ends. If you delete your account, your name for buddies, your buddy pairings, invites, suggestions to run together, weekly summaries, cheers, reports and race lists are deleted, and your buddies only see "Your buddy has left".

Running group

Running group: in a group you joined yourself, the other members see the name you chose, whether you are the group's admin and, while you share your week in the group, how many sessions you have planned and done this week and how many weeks in a row you have followed the plan; your done sessions and how far you have run this month then count towards the group's monthly goal as a total for everyone who shares, never your own runs. The group never sees health data, heart rate, HRV, sleep, weight, workouts, places, routes or the coach's texts. When at least three other members who share their week have the same race in their plans, you can see the race's name and how many in the group are running it, never who; you only see this for races in your own plan. The coach may suggest a shared goal for the month from anonymous, combined figures about the group (number of members, how many sessions they usually plan and do, last month's total sessions and distance). Only figures from members who share their week and have agreed to the coach are counted, and only when there are at least three such members and the group's admin has agreed to the coach and has Mila Coach; otherwise nothing about the group is sent. These are sent to Mila's coach, an external AI service in the USA, without names, the group's name or any single member's numbers. The group's admin decides the goal, in sessions, distance or both. Members can cheer with a ready-made message or up to 80 characters of their own text without links, with a daily limit, and the notification only says that someone cheered. Group names are filtered. You can report a group's name, a cheer or a member (the admin too) to the Mila team and block a member; the block applies in all groups and for running buddies, and the blocked person is not told. The coach may be told that you are in a running group and how many of its members' planned sessions were done that week, and may mention it in the weekly review; never who the members are. Only other members who share their week and have agreed to the coach are counted, and only when there are at least three of them; otherwise the coach is told nothing about the group. Whoever creates the group is its admin and can invite, remove members, rename it, set the month's goal and hand the role over. You can leave a group at any time. If you delete your account, your memberships, cheers, reports and monthly figures are deleted; if the admin leaves, the role passes to the longest-standing member, and a group without members is deleted. Free text you send to other runners (a cheer in your own words, the name you choose for buddies and group names) is reviewed automatically before it is delivered, with the help of an external AI service in the USA if you have allowed the coach to use AI, otherwise with a word list. A text that is held is not delivered; it is kept for at most 30 days so the Mila team can check it, and is then deleted. It is deleted at once if your account or the recipient's is deleted.

Public loops

Public loops (part of Mila Coach): your loops under My loops are private and stay on your phone. You can make a loop public yourself, one at a time, once you have run it at least three times. Before it becomes public, the app tells you that the whole loop is shown as you ran it, with its start and finish, and you confirm. The loop's route from one of your runs, simplified to about ten metres, is then stored with its length and climb on Mila's server (in the EU), linked to your account so that only you can change or remove it. Other Mila runners nearby may see the loop in the app when it suits their session. They see the whole loop, but never your name, when you ran it, your pace or how often you ran it. Mila never shows who made a loop. If a loop starts where you live, where it starts is visible, but not that it is you. Make loop private removes it from the server at once, and it is no longer shown to anyone. To find public loops nearby, your phone fetches the loops for a larger area, roughly 50 × 30 km per tile, and picks the nearby ones itself. Your location is not sent. A runner who finds a loop wrong or unsafe can report it to the Mila team, which can remove it from suggestions. If you are suspended from Running buddy and Running group, your public loops are not shown either until the suspension is lifted. Public loops are never sent to the AI service and are deleted with your account.

Elevation for the race directory

Only if you turn on Contribute elevation to the race directory does the app send anything about elevation. The app asks once, when you have run a race from Mila's race directory; the setting is off from the start and is found under More → Watches and apps. With it on, after such a race the app sends only the elevation profile along the race's course: the elevation your watch or phone measured, one height every 25 metres along the course (longer steps on ultra races), and whether an Apple Watch, a phone or something else measured it. No coordinates, times, heart rate or other details are sent, and a contribution is only taken for a race that is in your plan with its date passed. The heights are stored with a coded key made from your account and the race with a secret only Mila's server has, so that each runner counts once per race; the key does not say who you are and is different for every race. They are used only to give the race's course measured elevation instead of elevation from an elevation model or an uploaded file, in the app and on Mila's race pages, and only once several runners' measurements agree. To limit misuse, Mila counts how many contributions your account has sent in the last 24 hours; that count is deleted after nine days. If you delete your account, your contributions for the races in your plan are deleted with it; heights already published on a race's course stay, without any link to you. Turn the setting off under More → Watches and apps whenever you like, and nothing more is sent.

Location and weather

Your current location is used on the phone to fetch the weather forecast from Apple Weather and is not stored; your location and the name of the place are never sent to Mila's server or the AI service. (Only your runs' approximate height above sea level can be sent; see What leaves your phone.) In race mode and Long run with Mila, Mila on Apple Watch uses the watch's GPS for the route, which is saved with the workout in Apple Health, and in race mode to show where you are on the course and how far it is to the next climb, aid station and the finish; that position stays on the watch. Try race mode in race week uses the watch's GPS briefly to check that it finds you; nothing is recorded or saved. The forecast itself (temperature, rain and wind) is part of the summary the coach writes from. Workouts fetched from Polar include their GPS route, which is stored with the workout on your account until you disconnect or delete the account. The GPS route of a workout you import from a file is stored with the workout on your account until you delete the workout or the account. For a workout with GPS, the phone fetches the weather during the workout from Apple Weather, using the workout's start point rounded to about a kilometre and its time; that weather is kept only on the phone. The race-day weather for a race in your plan is fetched the same way, for the course's start rounded to about a kilometre: the weather for a race or a run never uses an exact position. Routes you run again are found on the phone from your workouts' routes, and the routes do not leave it; if you give such a route a name, the name is stored on your account with an id that is the id of the route's first workout, never any coordinates, and is deleted with the account. If you choose a contact for Check In on the Dark out card, the contact's name and phone number are stored only on your phone and sent to your own Apple Watch, never to Mila's server or the AI service; Check In itself is handled by Apple's Messages, and Mila never sees where you are.

Health data from Apple Health is used to provide the coaching in the app and otherwise only for what you choose yourself: to find your official result (your watch's finish time, sex and age class); how many sessions you have planned and done, which your running buddies and running groups see while you share your week, and which count towards the group's monthly goal and towards the anonymous, combined figures the coach gets to suggest it and, if you have agreed to the coach, to write your buddies' and group members' weekly reviews (see Running buddy and races and Running group); and the elevation you measured during a race, which you contribute to the race directory without any link to you (see Elevation for the race directory).

Automated decisions and AI

The coach's plans, changes to the plan and advice on your form are produced by an AI from your health data, your workouts, what you write and pictures you send. That is profiling, but no decisions with legal or similarly significant effects are made about you automatically: they are training suggestions, and you decide what you do. If you think a plan or an assessment is wrong, tell the coach, or ask for a person at Mila to look at it through the contact form. The app says clearly when you are chatting with an AI.

How long data is kept

Data is kept while you have an account. Coach texts older than two years may be deleted. Conversations with the AI support are deleted after 180 days; the copy of one forwarded to a person is kept in the support inbox for at most 90 days. Messages sent through the support form on Mila's website are kept in Mila's database for 90 days, with the e-mail address you give and one-way codes (hashes) of the sender's IP address and of its network to stop spam, and are then deleted. A report to the Mila team about a cheer, a group's name or a member is kept for 180 days from when it was made and then deleted, and earlier if the account that made it or that it concerns is deleted. The same goes for a report about a buddy request or a runner's name in a race's list, about a buddy's name, about a group's name before you joined, and about one of the coach's answers (with the copy of the answer). The Mila team reviews every report within 24 hours. Free text to other runners that the automatic review held is kept for at most 30 days and then deleted, and at once if your account or the recipient's is deleted. If the team shuts an account out of Running buddy and Running group, this is kept on the account and under a one-way code of the Apple sign-in, so it cannot be got round with a new account, until the team lifts it. When you accept the rules for Running buddy and Running group, when and which version you accepted is kept. When you delete your account under More → Account or on the subscription page, all data on your account is deleted from Mila's server immediately: description, coach texts, goal, what you told the coach under Tell your coach, connections, fetched workouts, imported and manually added workouts, ticked sessions, shoes, workout notes, races and courses you added, meals, support conversation and its forwarded copies, wishes, push addresses with their language, time zone and region, the Lock Screen's start address and usual wake time, and subscription status. A copy already e-mailed to the support inbox may remain there for up to 90 days. Two small records are kept apart from the account, under a one-way code of your Apple sign-in and nothing else: the App Store transaction id of a purchase together with that code, so the purchase is returned to the right person and cannot be taken over by someone else, and a count of the coach's free calls with the time you first signed up, so the coach's free start (its first days and free calls) cannot be repeated with a new account. They are kept as long as Mila is offered, at most five years from when they were recorded, and then deleted. Deleting the account does not cancel an App Store subscription: cancel it in iOS Settings → your name → Subscriptions. Mila cannot remove its own access at Polar: after you disconnect Polar or delete your account, remove Mila at account.polar.com. Courses that became part of the shared race registry remain without any link to you; a course still waiting for review is deleted with the account. To limit how much the coach can be used per person, Mila keeps a count of coach requests for nine days (29 days for running form analyses, and a year for the coach's race analysis of each race and for your first plan, so they are written once), linked to your account and to a one-way code derived from your Apple sign-in. If you delete your account, the link to the account is removed, but the count stays for the rest of those days so the limit cannot be avoided with a new account; it is then deleted. Under that one-way code alone, Mila also keeps how much text the coach's requests used per hour, for two days, for the same limit. Wishes are kept for at most one year, and for 90 days after the Mila team has marked them done; they are deleted with the account. A public loop is kept until you make it private or delete your account. Reports about loops are kept for 180 days. If the Mila team has removed a loop after a report, it is kept, shown to no one, as a record that it may not become public again, until you delete your account.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you may request access to your data (including who has received it), correction or deletion of it, or restriction of its processing, object to processing based on legitimate interest, and receive your data in a portable format. You can withdraw a consent at any time; that does not affect processing before the withdrawal. Contact us through the contact form or at support@milacoach.app (see Service provider details). You can also complain to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY), or to the data protection supervisory authority in the country where you live.

Legal basis

Mila processes health data on the basis of your explicit consent (GDPR art. 9(2)(a)), in two steps: that Mila collects, stores and analyses it (the screen Your health data, together with the iOS permission for Apple Health), and that it is sent to the coach, which is built on an external AI service (the screen A coach built on your own training), before anything is sent to the AI service. Your account, the subscription and the service itself are processed to perform our contract with you (art. 6(1)(b)). The counts that limit misuse, the one-way codes of the sender's IP address and network in the support form, the records that stop the free start or a purchase from being taken over with a new account, and stored support conversations are processed on the basis of Mila's legitimate interest in a secure service that is not misused and in being able to help you (art. 6(1)(f)). What Mila must keep by law, for example bookkeeping or answers to a request from an authority, is processed to comply with a legal obligation (art. 6(1)(c)). You can withdraw consent at any time: the health data under More → Account → Your health data, the coach under More → About you and your goal → Coach settings → The coach and AI (both also under Your consents on the subscription page the app shows without a subscription), Apple Health access in the Health app or in Settings, or everything by deleting your account under More → Account or on the subscription page.
