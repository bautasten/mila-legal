What Mila reads on your phone

With your permission Mila reads health data from Apple Health: workouts (with their route), heart rate, HRV, resting heart rate, VO₂max, sleep, blood oxygen, respiratory rate, sleeping wrist temperature, weight, height, body composition, activity, walking data, nutrition you log in other apps (energy, protein, carbohydrates and fat), and your date of birth and biological sex (Mila uses your age, not the date). This data is read and analysed on your phone. Mila on your phone never writes to Apple Health. Only when you use race mode in Mila on Apple Watch does the watch save the race as a workout in Apple Health, with its route, distance, active energy and heart rate; the watch sends it nowhere else, and it reaches the coach like any other run, through the summary. If you turn on sessions on Apple Watch, Mila puts the coach's runs in the Workout app on your watch; that happens on the phone and nothing about it goes through Mila's server. The same goes for the coach's sessions in your calendar if you turn that on: Mila writes only its own events to the calendar you choose and reads no others.

Apple Watch and Siri

If you have Mila on Apple Watch (the app or a complication on the watch face), your iPhone asks for today's note in the background in the morning, once last night's sleep is in Apple Health, so the coach's call is on your watch before you open the app. It is the same summary as when you open Today, and only if you have agreed to the AI coach and have Mila Coach. Between your iPhone and Apple Watch everything goes directly between your devices, not through Mila's server: today's and this week's sessions with the coach's call, today's and tomorrow's runs as workouts for the Workout app, the race plan in race week, the question after a run and your answer, and the coach's first sentence and stars about the run. Tell your coach on Apple Watch: what you dictate is turned into text by watchOS and sent to Mila on your iPhone, which handles the words exactly as when you type them in the app (they are saved on your account and included in the summary to the coach). The words stay on the watch until your iPhone has received them, for at most two days. If you ask Siri or Shortcuts about today's session, Mila answers from what is already on your iPhone or watch; nothing is sent to Mila's server or the AI service for it.

What leaves your phone

To write your coach texts, Mila sends a short summary of your training and health (for example weekly distance, recent workouts, form, recovery, sleep, blood oxygen, body measurements, age and sex, food you have logged and the day's weather forecast) together with your own description of yourself and your goal to Mila's server. The summary is used only to write the text: weekly texts keep it in a queue until they are written, at most about a day, and then it is cleared. The coach texts, your description, your goal and a coach profile derived from it are stored on your account. Sessions you tick off in the plan are stored on your account with their date, minutes and perceived effort (1–5), so the coach and all your phones see them; they are deleted with the account. If you move a session, the moved plan is stored on your account, and the plan and the move are sent to the external AI service so the coach can check the week. Exercises you tick off in a strength session and your watch choices (No watch, and whether the warm-up and cool-down go to Apple Watch as open or detailed steps) are stored on your account and deleted with the account. Your choices in the app for appearance (More → Appearance), units (More → Units) and the period the charts under Analysis show are stored on your account when you change them, so they follow you to a new phone; they are deleted with the account. What you write to the coach in your own words under Write to your coach that the coach finds concerns the week, and the coach's short interpretation of it (what has happened, a short summary and for how many days it affects your training), are stored on your account until you delete the account, and are sent to the external AI service as part of the summary the coach writes from. To decide whether what you write under Write to your coach concerns today's session or the week, your words are first sent to the external AI service with a short description of today's session; this is not stored. What you write there that concerns today's session is sent to the external AI service in the same way, with the day's summary, and your words, the coach's answer and your choice (use the suggestion or keep the plan) are stored on your account as a coach text for that day; if you use the suggestion, today's session in your plan is replaced. The days you tick off under This week's focus are stored on your account per week, with the coach's focus and how many days it set, so all your phones see them and the coach sees how it went in the weekly review; they are deleted with the account. Shoes you add (name, date, distance, replacement limit and the coach's details on the model), which run was done in which shoe and which shoe was your default, notes you write on your workouts, and races you add to your plan with the courses you upload for them are stored on your account, so they follow you to a new phone; they are deleted with the account. Phrases you write to yourself for the hard parts of a race are stored on your account with the race, so they follow you to a new phone, and are sent to Mila on Apple Watch with the race's plan; they are deleted with the race or the account. They are also sent to the external AI service as part of the summary when the coach judges how ready you are for the race, so the coach's suggested phrases can build on your own words. If you connect Polar, Mila stores the access tokens and, for the data types you approve at Polar: training sessions (with heart rate, speed and route), sleep (time asleep, sleep stages and sleep score), Nightly Recharge (night-time HRV, heart rate, breathing rate and recovery status), continuous heart rate (used for your daily resting heart rate), daily activity (steps and active time), night-time skin temperature, test results (orthostatic test, fitness test, SpO2) and diary entries in your Polar calendar (weight, feeling, perceived recovery and your physical settings such as maximum heart rate, resting heart rate, thresholds and VO2max). From your Polar physical settings Mila stores only max HR, resting HR, thresholds, VO₂max and FTP; birthday, sex and height are not read or stored. Mila does not read your e-mail, devices or diary notes at Polar, and cannot send anything to your Polar watch. If you connect COROS, you first agree in the app to what Mila fetches; Mila then stores the access tokens and, from your COROS account: workouts (time, activity, distance, energy, heart rate and pace, and the watch model), sleep (time asleep, sleep stages and sleep score), night-time HRV, resting heart rate, daily steps and your VO₂max. Mila does not read your COROS profile (height, weight, birthday, sex), your location or GPS tracks, stress or training plans, and does not send anything to your COROS watch. Data from COROS is shown with "Powered by COROS" and the watch model. When you disconnect COROS or delete your account, or when COROS no longer accepts Mila's access, Mila withdraws its access at COROS where it can and deletes all data fetched from COROS at once, in any case within 24 hours. If you allow notifications, Mila stores your phone's push address, with the phone's language, time zone and region, to tell you when weekly texts are ready (the language and time zone set the notification's text and time of day; the region is used to count users per country). The coach's notifications come between 08:00 and 21:00 on your phone; a reply from support (the AI support or a person at Mila) is sent as soon as it is ready, at any hour. For a race in your plan, Mila stores the race's name, date, start time and the coach's breakfast time on your account to send the race-week notifications (the evening before and on race morning); they are removed when the race has passed or left your plan, or when you turn race notifications off under More → Notifications, and are deleted with the account. Race courses you upload from a file or link are added to Mila's shared race registry, without your name. If you import workouts from files you exported yourself (FIT, GPX or TCX, for example Strava's archive), Mila reads the files on your phone and stores the workouts on your account: time, activity, distance, elevation gain, energy, heart rate (average, maximum and heart rate curve), speed and GPS route. The file itself is not sent to Mila's server and is not kept. Workouts you add manually are stored on your account with activity, time, distance, perceived effort (1–5) and your note. You can delete such a workout in the app; all of them are deleted with the account. Mila does not connect to Strava; workouts you record in Strava reach Mila through Apple Health or from files you export yourself.

Menstrual cycle (optional)

If you turn on "Let the coach take your menstrual cycle into account", Mila reads from Apple Health your periods (flow and cycle start), Apple Health's own cycle deviations (irregular, infrequent or prolonged periods and persistent spotting) and symptoms you have logged, such as cramps, headache, fatigue, hot flashes and night sweats. This is a separate, explicit consent to processing health data, apart from your agreement to the AI coach, off until you turn it on, and it applies only to the phone where you turn it on. The data is read and summarised on your phone; when the coach writes your daily texts and answers, only a short summary (roughly which day of your cycle you are on, your usual cycle length, Apple Health's deviations and today's and yesterday's symptoms) is sent to our external AI service in the USA, never the history or any dates. Mila does not store your cycle data, neither on your phone nor on our servers, beyond what the coach itself writes in its texts, which are stored like other coach texts. You can turn it off at any time under More → About you and your goal; Mila then immediately stops reading and sending the data and deletes what it had of it on your phone. You remove Mila's read access in the Health app under Sharing → Apps and Services → Mila. If you live in a US state with consumer health data laws, such as Washington's My Health My Data Act, your cycle data is consumer health data: Mila collects it only with your consent and only to tailor your coaching, shares it only with the AI service that writes the coach's texts on our behalf, never sells it, and you can withdraw your consent and request access or deletion at any time through the contact form at milacoach.app.

Subscription

If you subscribe to Mila Coach, Mila stores the App Store transaction id, the product, the status and the expiry date on your account to know whether the subscription is active. Apple sends Mila updates when the subscription renews, ends or is refunded. Payment details are handled by Apple and never reach Mila.

Meals and food

A meal photo is sent to the AI model for assessment and then discarded. Mila stores only the estimated numbers and text, never the photo. When you choose a photo, Mila reads when it was taken so the meal lands on the right day; the photo is sent scaled down without other metadata and is never saved. You can correct a logged meal in your own words, for example “half portion”. The correction is sent with the meal's saved parts, without any photo, to Mila's AI coach (an external AI service in the USA), and the meal is saved with the new estimate and your correction. With Siri or Shortcuts (“Log a meal in Mila”), what you say is sent as a description of the meal to Mila's AI coach, just as when you describe it in the app. Food you've logged in other apps that's in Apple Health (energy, protein, carbohydrate and fat per day) is added to your meals in Mila and included in the summary sent to the coach, including for the weekly review. If you choose Show without numbers, your food is shown without kcal and grams, and the coach then also writes about food without numbers. Log again copies an earlier meal without sending anything to the AI coach. The reminder about tonight's food is off until you turn it on under More → Notifications. It's a local notification on your phone with a generic text, never the coach's text, and it only comes when the coach has judged that tonight's food matters for tomorrow's session. Your own notes after long runs, for example about energy and your stomach, are part of what the coach uses for the weekly plan and the race's fuelling plan. In the weekly review the coach may say something brief about food when the pattern is clear over several logged days; it never suggests you eat less.

Shoes

If you import a shoe log under Shoes, the picture, or the text of the file you choose, is sent to the external AI service, which reads it to fill in your list of shoes. The picture is not stored, nor is the file's text; only the shoes you approve are stored on your account. To judge how long a shoe lasts for you, Mila also sends a summary of how the shoe is used (for example pace, distances and terrain of the runs in it), your weight, running dynamics from Apple Health when available, a summary of your latest running form analysis, what you have written about the shoe and the runs in it, and the shoe's age and distance. The coach's judgement and its reason are stored with the shoe on your account.

AI support

Messages you write to the support in the app are answered by an AI (an external AI service), and the answer says so. With your message the app sends a few technical details about the app and its connections (such as app version, iOS version and which services are connected), not your health data. The Mila team may read your support conversation, for up to 180 days, to help you and to improve the support. If the AI cannot help, or you ask for a person, a copy of the conversation is also e-mailed to Mila's support inbox. The conversation is stored on your account for 180 days and then deleted. If a person at Mila answers, the answer appears in the same conversation. When you delete your account, the conversation and the copies of it stored by Mila are deleted; a copy already e-mailed to the support inbox may remain there for up to 90 days.

Write to your coach

What you write under "Write to your coach" is sent, with a summary of your training data, to an external AI service. It first decides whether it is a question, a change to today's session or to the week, and then writes the answer. Questions and answers are kept only on your phone (the latest 20) and are deleted when you sign out; Mila's server keeps only the latest answer for about ten minutes, so it can be fetched again if the connection drops, and a count of how many questions you have asked. What you write about today's session or the week is stored on your account with the coach's answer or reading and is included in the coach's texts while it applies. If you leave the app before the answer is ready, it is kept until the app has fetched it, at most two days, and you get a notification without the answer in it.

Your body

If you write after a session that something hurts (under "Pain anywhere?", in the note or on the watch), it is sent with the session to the external AI service. If the coach judges it a niggle worth following, or follows your return after illness or injury, a short follow-up is stored on your account: the coach's line on how things stand, the coach's questions and your answers (at most the latest 14) and whether the coach has advised you to seek care. It is included in the coach's texts while it is open, is replaced when a new follow-up starts and is deleted when you delete your account. The coach never makes a diagnosis.

Your patterns and the year in review

Once a month the coach looks for patterns in your own data under Analysis → Recovery. A compact summary of the last weeks is then sent to the external AI service, the same kind of data the coach already receives: per day and week your runs (time of day, distance, time, pace, average heart rate, elevation gain, perceived effort and your notes), sleep, HRV and resting heart rate, together with your answers on whether last month's patterns were right. Those answers are kept only on your phone. For the coach's reflection in My running year (in December, or for the previous year when you open it) a summary of the year is sent the same way: distance, time, elevation gain and number of runs per month, races with times, records, the most-used shoe and weeks in a row with running. The patterns and the reflection are stored on your account like other coach texts. Since you can share the reflection, the coach writes it about your training only: it never mentions injuries, illness, weight, heart rate, sleep or other health details.

Shared year pages (My running year)

If you choose Share my year, Mila stores what you chose to show (for example total distance, time and elevation gain, races with times, records, your favourite shoe and, if you turn it on, the coach's reflection, which is then taken on the server from what the coach wrote for the year and never mentions health details) and a picture of your year made in the app, at an address that cannot be guessed. Heart rate, weight and other health values are never included. Anyone with the link can see the page; it is not indexed by search engines. The page is served through Cloudflare at ar.milacoach.app, which sees visitors' requests but nothing about your account; the page loads no fonts, scripts or anything else from third parties, and is never kept in a cache. You can have at most three active links per year and can stop sharing at any time in the app; the page then stops showing at once, and the page and the picture are removed entirely within a few minutes. Everything is deleted when you delete your account. Once a year, from 1 December, you get a notification about your running year so far. If you share your year before it ends and leave this on, the shared page is updated to the full year when you open the app from 1 January, under the same link and with the same details you chose to show, never more.

Running form analysis

If you ask the coach to analyse your running form, your films are read on your phone and never leave it. You can film in the app or choose videos from Photos. A video you take in the app is recorded without sound, is not saved to Photos and is deleted from your phone as soon as it has been read. The phone takes out still frames at key moments of your stride (at most 8 per angle), together with estimated measurements, and sends them to Mila's server. Before anything is sent, your phone crops each still frame around you and makes every face unrecognisable, including other people who happen to be in view. With the frames, Mila also sends, when there is any and you want to: the run the video was taken during (distance, pace and indoors or outdoors, found from the video's date), the shoe you choose, your watch's running-form measures from your latest runs, what you write about pace and surface, what the coach should know and how the drills have gone, and your previous analysis with the phone's measurements then. The frames wait in a queue until the analysis has been written, usually within a day and at most a few days if it has to be tried again, and are then deleted at Mila; the batch with them is deleted at the AI service once Mila has collected the analysis. The written analysis is stored on your account so the coach can compare it with the next one. The still frames and the body points the phone found in them are also kept on your phone, only there and never in a backup, so you can see what the coach looked at and compare with the next analysis. You can delete them under More → Running form; they are also deleted when you delete your account or sign in with another account. A short summary of your latest analysis (date, summary, main observations and drills) goes with the coach's other texts for twelve weeks after the analysis. The analysis is a coach's observations, not a medical assessment, and does not replace a running analysis in a lab. After an analysis, for the weeks it applies, the coach's weekly review gets your watch's running dynamics from Apple Health (cadence, ground contact time and vertical oscillation as weekly averages on easy runs), so the coach can say whether the drills seem to be working; this is sent as part of the summary.

After the race

When a race in your plan has been run, the coach writes an analysis of it: the summary sent to the external AI service then also holds the race's course, your time, your kilometre splits and heart rate during the race, the coach's plan for the race, your own notes on the race (also your answer on the watch after the run), the day's weather at the start and your earlier times in the same race; the analysis is stored on your account with the other coach texts. Mila can find your official result in two ways. Only if you have chosen to let Mila fetch your official results automatically (asked once on a race's page or when you add a race, and under More → About you and your goal), Mila sends, after each race in My races, the name you chose for results lists, with the race's name, place, date and distance, your watch's finish time for the race, your sex and age class from Apple Health and your club if you gave one, to the external AI service to find your own row in the organiser's or timing provider's public results list; Mila tries a few times in the two weeks after the race until the result is found. The name and the club are kept only on your phone and are not stored by Mila; you can change them or turn this off at any time. Without that choice nothing is sent unless you search yourself under Get my official result or Search yourself: then the name or bib number you type is sent, with the race's name, place, date and distance. The race always comes from your own race on your account, not from the app. The service first looks for the race's results list and then for your name or number in the pages it reads; the name may also be used in its web searches for that lookup. What you type, the name you chose and the other details are used only for that lookup and are not stored by Mila. If it is not certain which row is yours, up to three possible rows (time, placing and class only, never names) are shown on your phone for you to choose from; they are kept only on your phone until you choose. Mila stores only your result (official time, place overall and in your class, the class and the results page) on your account, never anyone else's, and it is deleted with the race or the account. When your result is found automatically you get a notification with the race's name, never more. A share image you make of a race or a workout is drawn on your phone and goes only where you send it. Heart rate, the coach's comment and the route are included from the start and can be turned off before you share. The backdrop is drawn on your phone too: to choose the landscape (for example city, lake or coast), the phone looks up the place a little way into the route through Apple's location service, and only the answer is kept, on the phone. When you open sharing, Mila asks the coach for a suggested text to go with the image (only if you have agreed to the AI coach): only what the image shows is sent to the external AI service (activity, date, distance, time, pace, elevation, the race's name and place, the official time and placing, the days left and forecast before a race, and a new record), never heart rate or other health data. When Coach's comment is on, the coach's own comment on the run, or after a race its race analysis, is also sent as the basis for a short line from the coach on the image; the line never shows health details. The suggestion and the line are stored on your account per image and language so they are not written again, and are deleted with the account. The text you share, which Mila also copies to the clipboard when you share, goes only where you send it.

Running buddy and races

Running buddy: once you and another Mila runner have both said yes, your buddy sees the name you chose for buddies and, while you share your week, how many sessions you have planned and done this week and how many weeks in a row you have followed the plan, and whether you have the same coming race in your plans (yes or no, never which). You can have at most ten running buddies. Your buddy never sees health data, heart rate, HRV, sleep, weight, workouts, places, routes or the coach's texts. The coach may be told that you have running buddies and how many of them completed the week's planned sessions, and may mention it in the weekly review; never who they are or anything else about them. On a race's page, Mila shows how many other Mila runners have the same race in their plan. The number is counted on the server without names and is only shown when it is at least five. If you turn on "Show me to other Mila runners in the race" (off by default), the name you chose for buddies is shown to other runners in the same race who also show themselves, and they can invite you to be running buddies. Nothing else about you is shown, and you can turn it off at any time. Cheers: buddies can send a ready-made message or up to 80 characters of their own text, and after a race runners who showed themselves can cheer each other once with a ready-made message. Cheers are kept for 30 days. The notification arrives between 08:00 and 21:00 and only says that someone cheered, never who or what. Your own text only goes between running buddies who have both said yes, without links, email addresses or phone numbers, and the number of cheers per day is limited. You can report a cheer to the Mila team, who then see what it said, and block the sender; the person you block is not told and can no longer invite you, cheer you on or see you in a race's list. You can end a buddy pairing at any time. If you delete your account, your name for buddies, your buddy pairings, invites, weekly summaries, cheers, reports and race lists are deleted, and your buddies only see "Your buddy has left".

Running group

Running group: in a group you joined yourself, the other members see the name you chose, whether you are the group's admin and, while you share your week in the group, how many sessions you have planned and done this week and how many weeks in a row you have followed the plan; your done sessions then count towards the group's monthly goal as a total. The group never sees health data, heart rate, HRV, sleep, weight, workouts, places, routes or the coach's texts. When at least three members who share their week have the same race in their plans, the group can see the race's name and how many are running it, never who; you only see this for races in your own plan. The coach may suggest a shared goal for the month from anonymous, combined figures about the group (number of members, how many sessions they usually plan and do, last month's total). These are sent to Mila's AI coach, an external AI service in the USA, without names, the group's name or any single member's numbers. The group's admin decides the goal. Members can cheer with a ready-made message or up to 80 characters of their own text without links, with a daily limit, and the notification only says that someone cheered. Group names are filtered. You can report a group's name, a cheer or a member (the admin too) to the Mila team and block a member; the block applies in all groups and for running buddies, and the blocked person is not told. The coach may be told that you are in a running group and how many of its members' planned sessions were done that week, and may mention it in the weekly review; never who the members are. Whoever creates the group is its admin and can invite, remove members, rename it, set the month's goal and hand the role over. You can leave a group at any time. If you delete your account, your memberships, cheers, reports and monthly figures are deleted; if the admin leaves, the role passes to the longest-standing member, and a group without members is deleted.

Elevation for the race directory

Only if you turn on Contribute elevation to the race directory does the app send anything about elevation. The app asks once, when you have run a race from Mila's race directory; the setting is off from the start and is found under More → Watches and apps. With it on, after such a race the app sends only the elevation profile along the race's course: the elevation your watch or phone measured, one height every 25 metres along the course (longer steps on ultra races), and whether an Apple Watch, a phone or something else measured it. No coordinates, times, heart rate or other details are sent. The heights are stored without any link to you or your account and are used only to give the race's course measured elevation instead of elevation from an elevation model, in the app and on Mila's race pages. To limit misuse, Mila counts how many contributions your account has sent in the last 24 hours; that count is kept apart from the heights and deleted after nine days. Since a contribution cannot be linked to you, it cannot be removed afterwards and stays if you delete your account; it goes with the race. Turn the setting off under More → Watches and apps whenever you like, and nothing more is sent.

Location and weather

Your current location is used on the phone to fetch the weather forecast from Apple Weather and is not stored; your location and the name of the place are never sent to Mila's server or the AI service. In race mode, Mila on Apple Watch uses the watch's GPS for the race's route, which is saved with the workout in Apple Health, and to show where you are on the course and how far it is to the next climb, aid station and the finish; that position stays on the watch. Try race mode in race week uses the watch's GPS briefly to check that it finds you; nothing is recorded or saved. The forecast itself (temperature, rain and wind) is part of the summary the coach writes from. Workouts fetched from Polar include their GPS route, which is stored with the workout on your account until you disconnect or delete the account. The GPS route of a workout you import from a file is stored with the workout on your account until you delete the workout or the account. For a workout with GPS, the phone fetches the weather during the workout from Apple Weather, using the workout's start point rounded to about a kilometre and its time; that weather is kept only on the phone. The race-day weather for a race in your plan is fetched the same way, for the course's start rounded to about a kilometre: the weather for a race or a run never uses an exact position. Routes you run again are found on the phone from your workouts' routes, and the routes do not leave it; if you give such a route a name, the name is stored on your account with an id that is the id of the route's first workout, never any coordinates, and is deleted with the account.

Sign in

You sign in with Apple. Mila receives an anonymous identifier from Apple and, if you choose to share it, your email address. Mila also keeps a token from Apple on the server, used only to withdraw the sign-in when you delete your account; it is deleted with the account. If Apple gives Mila your name, it is kept only on your phone (the first name for the app, and the full name as a suggestion for the name in results lists), as is any photo you choose under More → Account; none of them is sent to Mila's server.

Who processes the data

Supabase (database and server functions, hosted in the EU, Ireland) stores your account data. Only after you have agreed to it in the app, an external AI service (sub-processor) in the USA processes the training summary (including data from a connected Polar account: training sessions, sleep, Nightly Recharge, heart rate, activity and test results; data from a connected COROS account: workouts, sleep, night-time HRV, resting heart rate, steps and VO₂max; and workouts you import or add manually, with your note), your description, meal photos, meals you describe, race course files you add (start, finish and place), what you tell the coach about yourself and your workouts, and the still frames for a running form analysis (cropped around you, with every face made unrecognisable) and what you write with them to write coach texts, what you write under Write to your coach to answer it, and your messages to the AI support to answer them, as well as the name, place, date and distance of the races in your plan (nothing about you) to find the race organiser's official website and the race's official aid stations, and to summarise the results of recent years (up to five editions) from the race's public results lists into the number of finishers and finishing times (no names are stored), which the coach compares with your forecast and, for your class, with your age and sex to estimate your placing; the same details of a race that is not yet in Mila's race registry are also used to find the race and the course the organiser has published, which are then added to the registry with no link to you; and the name, place, date and distance of a race in your plan (nothing about you) to find the start times, start place, bib pickup, cut-off times and mandatory kit the organiser has published for it; and, only when you ask for your official result or have chosen to let Mila fetch it automatically after your races, the name or bib number you type or the name you chose, with the race's name, place, date and distance and, when fetched automatically, your watch's finish time, your sex and age class and your club if you gave one, which the AI service may use in its web searches to find your result (Mila does not store the name or these details); the AI service does not use the data to train its models. It keeps the data only for a short time under its contract with Mila, to detect misuse, and then deletes it. The transfer to the USA is protected by the EU's standard contractual clauses in the AI service's data processing terms. Weekly texts are written through the AI service's batch service, and each batch is deleted there once Mila has collected the texts. How long data is kept

Data is kept while you have an account. Coach texts older than two years may be deleted. Conversations with the AI support are deleted after 180 days; the copy of one forwarded to a person is kept in the support inbox for at most 90 days. Messages sent through the support form on Mila's website are kept in Mila's database for 90 days, with the e-mail address you give and a one-way code (hash) of the sender's IP address to stop spam, and are then deleted. When you delete your account under More → Account, all data on your account is deleted from Mila's server immediately: description, coach texts, goal, what you told the coach under Write to your coach, connections, fetched workouts, imported and manually added workouts, ticked sessions, shoes, workout notes, races and courses you added, meals, support conversation and its forwarded copies, push addresses with their language, time zone and region, and subscription status. Two small records are kept apart from the account, under a one-way code of your Apple sign-in and nothing else: the App Store transaction id of a purchase together with that code, so the purchase is returned to the right person and cannot be taken over by someone else, and a count of the coach's free calls with the time you first signed up, so the coach's free start (its first days and free calls) cannot be repeated with a new account. If you delete your account, the link to the account is removed, but the count stays for the rest of those days so the limit cannot be avoided with a new account; it is then deleted.
